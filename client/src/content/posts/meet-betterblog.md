You have a Squarespace site and it looks *great*. You chose custom fonts. You agonized over the exact shade of off-white. Then you clicked over to your blog, and it looked like it was built by a different, sadder website platform.

If you've noticed this and wondered why, it's not just you. The Squarespace forum is full of people saying the same thing. This July a Platinum Partner titled a thread "Once again, here is my plea for Squarespace to give us better blog features" and put the important part in capitals: WE. NEED. BETTER. BLOGGING. FEATURES. Squarespace 7.1 gives you a perfectly nice online journal and not much more. It has no sidebar; those stayed behind in 7.0. Related posts are something you build by hand, with a Summary Block, on every single post. Pick a category and the whole page reloads with no sign anything changed. Every post gets exactly one author. And your whole archive hides behind one button, labeled Older Posts, which you press again and again like an elevator that only goes down one floor at a time.

Also not included: a table of contents, reading time, lead magnets, a last-updated date, or any real idea which posts people actually finish reading.

We've spent years patching these issues on client sites with custom code. BetterBlog is what happened when we got tired of patching.

It's available now.

## What it is

BetterBlog is an add-on for Squarespace 7.1 blogs. You paste one script into Code Injection, once. From then on you run your blog's design from the BetterBlog dashboard: pick a layout, flip on the features you want, check it on your real blog, in your own fonts, on desktop, tablet and phone, then save.

Now the nerdy part, which is our favorite part. Squarespace already publishes every page on your site as structured data. Add `?format=json` to the end of almost any Squarespace URL and look. (Go ahead. We'll wait.) BetterBlog reads that same data for your blog and redraws it in the layout you picked, using the fonts and colors you already set on your site. Your posts don't move. Your URLs don't change. There is nothing to migrate, because nothing leaves Squarespace.

You keep writing in Squarespace exactly the way you do now. We change what the blog looks like when it shows up.

## Ten templates. Twenty-five combinations. Forty layouts.

Five templates for the blog page, five for the posts. Any blog page template pairs with any post template, which makes 25 combinations.

(Why forty? Every template was designed for desktop and mobile, with and without a paywall. We counted. Then we said a word we can't print here.)

For the blog page:

- **The Showcase:** big alternating images. For when the photos are the point.
- **The Newsroom:** a compact list with search. Dense, fast, scannable.
- **The Masthead:** one large hero over an image grid. The front-page treatment.
- **The Editorial:** an alternating mosaic with text on the images. The magazine one.
- **The Digest:** a main column with a sidebar. Yes, a sidebar. In 7.1.

For posts:

- **The Reporter:** title beside the image, sidebar on the right.
- **The Feature:** centered header, full-width hero image.
- **The Writer:** centered header, no image. Just the words.
- **The Story:** a dark split hero above a narrow reading column. Moody.
- **The Publisher:** title over a hero image, sidebar on the right.

Every one of them picks up the fonts and colors from your Squarespace site, so the blog finally looks like it belongs to the site around it. You set the title, subtitle and byline sizes yourself, from small to extra large, and how the header image sits.

Essentials comes with four templates (The Showcase and The Digest for the blog page, The Feature and The Reporter for posts). Professional has eight. Publication has all ten, including the two magazine layouts, The Editorial and The Story. You can preview all ten on any plan. On Professional and Publication you can run more than one blog on a site, each with its own template. A wedding journal and a set of portrait guides don't have to look alike.

## The switches

Each template has its own set of features, and you turn on the ones you want. Across the ten:

**For readers:** a table of contents, reading time, a "last updated" date on posts you've revised, a progress bar that fills as they scroll, breadcrumbs, related and popular posts, previous and next links, share buttons.

**For finding things:** search for your blog, tag and category filters, sorting, featured and pinned posts, and numbered pages. Page 1, 2, 3, 4. Readers can see how deep the archive goes and jump straight to the part they want. No more Older Posts.

**For authors and commenters:** more than one author on a post (Squarespace allows exactly one), author bio boxes with real formatting (bold, links, lists), and threaded comments you moderate from the dashboard, on every plan. Readers can comment anonymously, or confirm their email and comment as a verified member. You choose which.

**For email lists and members-only posts:** sign-up forms and lead magnets on your posts, every address collected in one place and exportable as a CSV, or sent straight to your Kit or Mailchimp list. If you sell memberships through Squarespace, BetterBlog marks locked posts on the blog page and shows logged-out readers a membership prompt on the post itself.

**For knowing what works:** how far people actually read, which links get clicked, what people type into your blog's search, and how each author's posts perform. Real numbers, per post, in your dashboard.

Not every template has every feature. Sidebars live in The Digest, The Reporter and The Publisher, and you choose how each one shows up on phones. Some features come with the higher plans, and the [pricing page](/#pricing) lists what's where.

## The fine print

- BetterBlog works with Squarespace 7.1.
- It needs Code Injection, which means a Core plan or higher.
- It changes how your blog looks and works on your live site. It doesn't touch Squarespace's post editor. You still write in the classic editor, and we still think that's where the words belong.
- Plans start at $9 a month, billed annually. Every plan has a 7-day free trial. We ask for a card up front and don't charge it until the trial ends.
