# Templates

Templates are the fastest way to set up a great-looking blog. Each template is a curated layout — a carefully chosen combination of settings that work well together — applied in one click. BetterBlog has five collection templates and five post templates, each with a distinct visual feel and feature set.

Templates are the starting point, not a cage. After applying one, you can adjust any setting the template doesn't control. If you want a different starting point later, switching templates is always available.

---

## How templates work

Every blog in BetterBlog always has a template active — one for the collection page and one for post pages. When you first connect a blog, **The Showcase** (collection) and **The Feature** (post) are applied by default.

**What a template controls:**
Each template owns a specific subset of settings — layout, sidebar configuration, featured post placement, footer modules, and so on. Settings the template controls are hidden from the customization panel, because the template is making those decisions for you. Settings the template doesn't control remain fully editable.

This means two things practically:
- You'll see different options available in the panel depending on which template is active
- Switching templates may reveal or hide different controls

**Applying a template:**
Open the BetterBlog customizer, click **Change Template**, and choose from the template picker. Each template shows a layout preview and a short description. The currently active template is highlighted with a purple border.

When you click a template to apply it, a confirmation dialog appears before anything changes. Confirming overwrites your current settings for that level (collection or post) with the template's defaults. This cannot be undone — if you've made customizations you want to keep, note them before switching.

Collection and post templates are independent. Switching your collection template doesn't affect your post template, and vice versa.

## Collection templates

Collection templates control how your blog's index page looks — how posts are listed, whether a featured article is shown, how images are displayed, and which discovery modules (search, filter, sort) are available.

---

### The Showcase
**Large alternating images**

The Showcase is a bold, image-forward layout. Posts alternate sides as you scroll down — image on the left, then right, then left — giving the collection a magazine-like rhythm. It works best for blogs where the featured image is a key part of each post.

Best for: photography blogs, travel writing, visual storytelling.

**What this template controls:**
- Full-bleed alternating image layout
- Featured article position (in-layout, top of grid)
- Infinite scroll (no numbered pagination)
- No sidebars

**What you can customize:**
- Show Date, Reading Time, Author, Excerpt toggles
- Email Capture and Lead Magnet footer modules
- Featured post selection (automatic or pinned)

---

### The Newsroom
**Compact list with search**

The Newsroom is a dense, text-forward layout. Posts appear as compact rows — title, date, and a short excerpt — making it easy to scan a large archive quickly. Search is built into the header by default, making it the strongest template for content-heavy blogs with a lot of posts.

Best for: news-style blogs, newsletters, high-volume publishing.

**What this template controls:**
- Compact list layout with left-justified thumbnails
- Numbered pagination
- Search module in the header
- Featured article position (in-layout)

**What you can customize:**
- Show Date, Reading Time, Author toggles
- Filter and Sort modules
- Email Capture and Lead Magnet footer modules
- Featured post selection

---

### The Masthead
**Hero plus image grid**

The Masthead opens with a large hero feature article, then drops into an image grid below. The header includes filter, sort, and search by default — it's the most discovery-rich template out of the box. Email Capture in the footer is on by default.

Best for: lifestyle blogs, brand blogs, publications with editorial curation.

**What this template controls:**
- Hero featured article in the header
- Image grid layout below the hero
- Infinite scroll
- Filter, Sort, and Search modules in the header
- Email Capture in the footer (on by default)
- Pagination mode (locked to infinite scroll)

**What you can customize:**
- Show Date, Reading Time, Author, Excerpt toggles
- Lead Magnet footer module
- Featured post selection

---

### The Editorial
**Alternating mosaic, text on images**

The Editorial creates a mosaic of large overlapping image cards, with post titles and metadata displayed directly on top of the images. It's a visually striking layout best suited to blogs with strong photography and short, punchy post titles.

Best for: arts and culture blogs, design studios, curated content.

**What this template controls:**
- Alternating mosaic card layout
- Text-on-image post cards
- Numbered pagination
- Excerpts hidden (not available to enable on this template)
- Featured article position (in-layout)
- Footer modules off by default

**What you can customize:**
- Show Date, Reading Time, Author toggles
- Filter, Search, Sort modules
- Email Capture and Lead Magnet footer modules
- Featured post selection

---

### The Digest
**Main column with sidebar**

The Digest is the most traditional blog layout — a main content column with a persistent right sidebar. The sidebar includes author profiles, email capture, popular posts, and filter by default. It's the only collection template with a sidebar enabled out of the box.

Best for: personal blogs, writing-focused publications, readers who expect a classic blog feel.

**What this template controls:**
- Main column + right sidebar layout
- Sidebar modules: author profiles, email capture, popular posts, category filter
- Featured article position (in-layout)
- Numbered pagination

**What you can customize:**
- Show Date, Reading Time, Author, Excerpt toggles
- Sidebar width and sticky behavior
- Sort and Search modules in the header (not the sidebar)
- Featured post selection

## Post templates

Post templates control how individual blog posts are displayed — the header layout, whether sidebars appear, which footer modules are included, and features like the progress bar and table of contents.

---

### The Reporter
**Title beside image, right sidebar**

The Reporter places the post title and metadata to the left of the featured image in the header, then drops into a single-column body with a right sidebar. The progress bar is on by default. The right sidebar includes author profiles and related posts. A lead magnet appears in the footer.

Best for: journalism-style blogs, news posts, content where byline and metadata matter.

**What this template controls:**
- Title left-of-image header layout
- Right sidebar with author profiles and related posts
- Progress bar (on by default)
- Lead Magnet in footer

**What you can customize:**
- Show Date, Reading Time, Author toggles
- Progress bar color, thickness, and position
- Social sharing links
- Email Capture in footer

---

### The Feature
**Centered header, full-bleed hero**

The Feature is the default post template. The featured image fills the full width of the viewport with the post title overlaid on top. Below the image, the post body sits in a centered column flanked by two sidebars — the table of contents on the left, and an author/related posts module on the right. Social sharing is on by default.

Best for: long-form writing, essays, feature articles where the reading experience is the priority.

**What this template controls:**
- Full-bleed hero header with centered title overlay
- Left sidebar: Table of Contents
- Right sidebar: author profiles and related posts
- Social sharing (on by default)
- Rich footer with previous/next navigation

**What you can customize:**
- Show Date, Reading Time, Author toggles
- TOC style (numbered, connected dots, bookmark)
- Progress bar
- Email Capture and Lead Magnet in footer

---

### The Writer
**Centered header, no image**

The Writer strips everything back to just the words. No featured image, no sidebars, no decorative elements — just a clean, centered column with the post title, byline, and body. Author profiles and previous/next navigation appear in the footer. It's the most minimal post template.

Best for: essays, personal writing, newsletters where the image is not important.

**What this template controls:**
- No featured image (image hidden regardless of post settings)
- No sidebars
- Full-screen footer margins
- Author profiles and previous/next in footer

**What you can customize:**
- Show Date, Reading Time, Author toggles
- Social sharing links
- Email Capture and Lead Magnet in footer

---

### The Story
**Dark split hero, narrow column**

The Story opens with a dark-background header — the featured image on one side, the post title in large type on the other — then flows into a narrow reading column below. It's the most visually distinctive post template, best suited to editorial-quality writing with a strong featured image.

Best for: literary blogs, opinion writing, posts that open with an image worth showcasing.

**What this template controls:**
- Dark split-hero header with configurable background color
- Narrow reading column
- No sidebars
- Table of Contents locked off
- Social sharing on by default

**What you can customize:**
- Header background color
- Show Date, Reading Time, Author toggles
- Social sharing platforms
- Email Capture and Lead Magnet in footer

---

### The Publisher
**Hero overlay, right sidebar**

The Publisher uses a full-bleed hero image with the post title overlaid at the bottom, then flows into a body column with a right sidebar containing popular posts, related content, and filter options. It's the most structured post template — closest to a traditional editorial publication layout.

Best for: multi-author blogs, content-heavy sites, posts that are part of a larger archive.

**What this template controls:**
- Full-bleed hero with title overlay at bottom
- Right sidebar: popular posts, related posts, category filter
- Author profiles and related posts in footer

**What you can customize:**
- Show Date, Reading Time, Author toggles
- Progress bar
- Social sharing links
- Email Capture and Lead Magnet in footer

## Switching templates

You can switch templates at any time. Open the customizer, click **Change Template**, and select a new one from the picker. Before the new template is applied, a confirmation dialog will appear — confirm to proceed.

**Switching overwrites your current settings for that level.** Any customizations you've made (show/hide toggles, sidebar widths, footer module copy, etc.) will be replaced by the new template's defaults. Make a note of anything you want to re-apply afterward.

Your posts, content, analytics, leads, and comment data are never affected by template changes.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use a collection template without a post template?**
No — both levels always have a template active. You can mix and match freely though: any collection template works with any post template.

**Can I go back to a custom layout without any template?**
Not in the current version. Every blog uses a template at each level. If you want maximum flexibility, The Digest (collection) and The Feature (post) expose the most customizable settings of their respective template sets.

**Will switching templates affect my live blog immediately?**
Yes. Changes take effect as soon as you confirm the template switch in the customizer.

**A setting I was using has disappeared after switching templates. Where did it go?**
The new template controls that setting, so it's been hidden from the panel. Switching to a different template may expose it again, or you can work within the current template's customizable options.

**The preview in the template picker doesn't look exactly like my live blog. Why?**
The previews show layout structure, not your actual content. Your blog's fonts, colors, images, and Squarespace styling will all carry through to the live result — the preview is a schematic of the layout, not a pixel-perfect render.
